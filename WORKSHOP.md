# Workshop and hackathon starter (90 minutes)

A format for getting a mixed room (students, developers, people from government or business) from zero to a working AI prototype. It works in Arabic, English or both.

## Before the session

* Run `python bench.py` with the model you plan to use. Check it answers in the right language and note the latency people will see.
* Prepare one shared endpoint (or Ollama on each laptop) and test it on the venue Wi-Fi.
* Print the three challenge cards below in Arabic and English.
* Make a short feedback form (questions at the bottom of this page).

## Agenda

| Time | Block | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 10 | What a foundation model is, in plain words | Everyone shares the same picture |
| 10 to 30 | First call: run `chat.py` or `chat.mjs` | Every participant gets a real answer from a model |
| 30 to 60 | Build: teams pick a challenge card | A working prototype per team |
| 60 to 80 | Demos, two minutes per team | Teams explain what worked and what broke |
| 80 to 90 | Feedback form and next steps | Collect data, invite people to the next session |

## Challenge cards

1. **Arabic FAQ helper.** Give the model a school or service FAQ and make it answer parents' questions in Arabic.
2. **Small business assistant.** Draft product descriptions in Arabic and English for a home business.
3. **Explain it simply.** Turn a technical paragraph into an explanation for a 12 year old, in both languages.

Stretch goal for every team: run `bench.py` on their own prompts and report one thing the model got wrong.

## What to measure

* Share of participants who got a first answer within 20 minutes
* Number of working demos at the end
* Language mismatches and wrong terms found by the teams
* Feedback score and the top three requests
* People who come back to the next session or keep building after it

## Feedback form questions

1. Did you get the model to answer you? (yes / no)
2. Did it answer in the language you asked in?
3. What did you build, in one sentence?
4. What stopped you or confused you?
5. What would you want to build next time?
